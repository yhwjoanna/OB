Last Friday night, nobody was on the on-call schedule

What Happened
P1 Incident ── 87 min
What happened

Friday night was the scheduled database upgrade window. After the upgrade, the connection pool settings were not adjusted to match. The order service ran out of connections within 20 minutes, and payment integration failed with it.

About 400 enterprise clients were affected and could not place orders that night, which triggered the availability clauses in their contracts. Customer Success escalated the matter to the VP of Engineering.

03

Three metrics, usually only two can be held

How to Play
3 Metrics
Metric 01

Task progress

Getting the work done

Tickets closed on time, the on-call schedule filled, and the change and on-call records the audit needs ready to hand over. This is what the organization pays salaries for.

Warning sign: The audit samples a blank on-call record, or the next incident hits and no one can take it right away.
Metric 02

Team trust

Keeping the team together

Whether colleagues keep backing each other up and whether they stay. When trust hits bottom, people start leaving, and usually several at once.

Warning sign: Members start bypassing internal scheduling to go straight to others, or fill out a transfer request and send it in.
Metric 03

Personal capital

Your standing and energy

Yen-ting Chen's standing on the team and in the company, plus how much energy he has left. Once capital runs out, none of the later moves gets any traction.

Warning sign: Extra work never makes it into any record, and at the calibration meeting he ends up rated as needing improvement.

Getting it done vs. keeping trust

Behfar and Golberg point out that what makes or breaks a team is not whether it has conflict but how it handles conflict. Most teams manage only one side, either getting the work done or looking after members' feelings; the few effective teams deliberately design ways to do both.

Take home four questions

Debrief
4 Questions
Q 01

Whose problem is it?

Throughout the simulation, Po-jui's performance problem is handled as a peer problem. Looking one level up, if one person can perform poorly for three straight years with no consequences, which management mechanisms have failed? List at least three.

Q 02

Delegating vs. passing the buck

In Round 4, the team leader hands responsibility for managing a person to a subordinate with no appraisal authority. What separates delegation from passing the buck? Of responsibility, authority, resources and accountability, which were actually handed over and which stayed where they were?

Q 03

The cost of going over the boss's head

Under what conditions is reporting past a direct supervisor responsible behavior, and under what conditions does it just shift the risk onto the person reporting? Judge along three dimensions: customer impact, strength of evidence, and existing grievance channels.

Q 04

If you were Team Leader Huang

Suppose senior management really did hint that Po-jui should be left alone. Within that constraint, what can a manager still do without confronting senior management? List three concrete actions and explain the risk of each.
