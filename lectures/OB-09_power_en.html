What Is Power?

WHAT IS POWER?
[ DEFINITION ]
Power refers to a capacity that A has to influence the behavior of B so that B acts in accordance with A's wishes. Power may exist without being used.
+ [ 01/CAPACITY ]

Power is a potential

A CAPACITY

Power is a potential for influence; it can exist without being exercised. A person can hold power and choose not to use it. Its most important aspect is this latent capacity.

+ [ 02/DEPENDENCY ]

The essence of power: dependence

DEPENDENCY

The most important aspect of power is that it is a function of dependence. A has power over B only if A controls something B desires. The greater the dependence, the greater the power; this logic runs through the whole chapter.

Power vs. Leadership

POWER VS. LEADERSHIP
[ 3 CONTRASTS ]
+ [ 01/GOALS ]

Goal compatibility

GOAL COMPATIBILITY

Leadership requires some congruence between the goals of the leader and those led; power does not, as long as dependence exists. Power can serve the organization or private interests.

+ [ 02/DIRECTION ]

Direction of influence

DIRECTION OF INFLUENCE

Leadership research focuses on downward influence on followers; power research covers upward, downward, and lateral influence.

+ [ 03/FOCUS ]

Research emphasis

RESEARCH EMPHASIS

Leadership research mostly emphasizes leader style; power research focuses on tactics for gaining compliance and on dependence relationships.

Bases of Power

BASES OF POWER
[ 2 CATEGORIES ]
+ [ 01/FORMAL ]

Formal power

FORMAL POWER

Based on an individual's position in an organization; includes coercive, reward, and legitimate power. Formal power comes and goes with the position.

+ [ 02/PERSONAL ]

Personal power

PERSONAL POWER

Comes from personal characteristics, expertise, and the respect and admiration of others; includes expert and referent power. It stays with the person after leaving the position.

Three Types of Formal Power

FORMAL POWER
[ 3 TYPES ]
+ [ 01/COERCIVE ]

Coercive power

COERCIVE POWER

Depends on fear: people comply because they fear the negative results of not complying, such as discipline, transfer, or dismissal. It also includes covert means such as withholding key information.

+ [ 02/REWARD ]

Reward power

REWARD POWER

People comply because doing so produces benefits they value, such as pay raises, promotions, or desirable work assignments. Reward power is the mirror image of coercive power: one offers the carrot, the other the stick.

+ [ 03/LEGITIMATE ]

Legitimate power

LEGITIMATE POWER

The formal authority to control and use organizational resources; it is the broadest of the formal power bases. Members who accept the hierarchy of authority accept the basis of legitimate power.

Two Types of Personal Power

PERSONAL POWER
[ 2 TYPES ]
+ [ 01/EXPERT ]

Expert power

EXPERT POWER

Influence wielded as a result of expertise, special skill, or knowledge. It comes from a person's professional competence and experience. In a knowledge economy, expert power keeps growing in weight.

+ [ 02/REFERENT ]

Referent power

REFERENT POWER

Based on identification with a person who has desirable resources or personal traits; it develops out of admiration of and respect for that person. Celebrity endorsements are a commercial use of referent power.

Which Bases of Power Are Most Effective?

WHICH BASES ARE MOST EFFECTIVE?
[ 4 FINDINGS ]
+ [ 01 ]

Personal power works best

Expert and referent power are positively related to employee satisfaction, organizational commitment, and performance; they are the most effective bases.

+ [ 02 ]

Formal power has limits

Reward and legitimate power seem to be unrelated to these outcomes; power that comes with a position does not buy genuine commitment and identification.

+ [ 03 ]

Coercive power backfires

Coercive power is negatively related to employee satisfaction and commitment.

+ [ 04 ]

Referent power matters

Referent power is a powerful motivator.

Dependence: The Key to Power

DEPENDENCE: THE KEY TO POWER
[ 3 POINTS ]
+ [ 01/POSTULATE ]

The basic postulate

GENERAL DEPENDENCE POSTULATE

The greater B's dependence on A, the more power A has over B. This general rule is the starting point for understanding power relationships in organizations.

+ [ 02/CONTROL ]

Resource control

RESOURCE CONTROL

When a person alone controls resources that others desire, others become dependent on that person. Exclusive control is the key: a resource others can bypass yields no power.

+ [ 03/ALTERNATIVES ]

Alternatives

ALTERNATIVES

The more options people have, the less they can be controlled by others. A broad set of skills and contacts is the best protection against domination.

What Creates Dependence?

WHAT CREATES DEPENDENCE?
[ 3 FACTORS ]
+ [ 01/IMPORTANCE ]

Importance

IMPORTANCE

How important the resource is to others. If nobody wants what you control, there is no dependence. Units that reduce an organization's key uncertainties hold the most power.

+ [ 02/SCARCITY ]

Scarcity

SCARCITY

Dependence rises with the scarcity of a resource, as the relative power of different occupations shows. Skills in short supply in the labor market command stronger bargaining power.

+ [ 03/NONSUB ]

Nonsubstitutability

NONSUBSTITUTABILITY

The fewer viable substitutes for a resource, the more power its controller has. Advances in AI are making some skills increasingly substitutable.

Formal Small-Group Networks

FORMAL SMALL-GROUP NETWORKS
[ 3 TYPES ]
Formal organizational networks can be very complicated, and information flowing through them has major implications for power and influence. Common small-group networks condense into three types.
+ [ 01/CHAIN ]

Chain

CHAIN

Rigidly follows the formal chain of command; it approximates the communication channels of a rigid three-level organization.

+ [ 02/WHEEL ]

Wheel

WHEEL

Relies on a central figure as the conduit for all communication; it approximates a work group with a strong leader. The central figure gains information, support, and greater power and status.

+ [ 03/ALL-CHANNEL ]

All-channel

ALL-CHANNEL

All members actively communicate with each other; common in self-managed teams, with no single leader. Many organizations treat it as the ideal.

Social Network Analysis

SOCIAL NETWORK ANALYSIS
[ 4 POINTS ]
+ [ 01/DEFINITION ]

Definition

Social network analysis is a tool for assessing resource exchanges and dependencies within an organization.

+ [ 02/FUNCTION ]

Function

It examines communication patterns among members to identify how information flows. The connections between nodes are called ties; people who exchange resources often have strong ties.

+ [ 03/SOCIOGRAM ]

Sociogram

A sociogram diagrams the relationships among individuals in a social network, much like an informal version of an organization chart.

+ [ 04/BROKER ]

Comparison and brokers

A formal chart shows how authority is supposed to flow; a sociogram shows how resources actually flow. Brokers gain power by controlling unique resources from different groups.

Power Tactics (1)

POWER TACTICS
[ 01-04/09 ]
Power tactics are the specific ways individuals translate power bases into action to influence others; they are key tools for managers. There are nine main power tactics.
+ [ 01/LEGITIMACY ]

Legitimacy

LEGITIMACY

Relying on one's authority position or organizational policies to demand compliance; typical in formal structures, and its effect depends on the strength of position power.

+ [ 02/RATIONAL ]

Rational persuasion

RATIONAL PERSUASION

Presenting logical arguments and factual evidence; the most widespread and professional means of influence, especially suited to expert decision processes.

+ [ 03/INSPIRATIONAL ]

Inspirational appeals

INSPIRATIONAL APPEALS

Appealing to others' values, ideals, and aspirations to build enthusiasm and commitment; especially suited to driving change or major projects.

+ [ 04/CONSULTATION ]

Consultation

CONSULTATION

Involving others in the decision process and inviting their advice and input, which raises acceptance of and commitment to the decision.

Power Tactics (2)

POWER TACTICS
[ 05-09/09 ]
ExchangeEXCHANGE
Offering benefits or reciprocal favors in exchange for support; it rests on mutual benefit, but the exchange must be fair.
Personal appealsPERSONAL APPEALS
Asking for support based on friendship or personal ties; it relies on good relationships, but use it with care to avoid exploiting friendship.
IngratiationINGRATIATION
Using praise or friendly behavior to win favor; it must be sincere, and overuse can backfire.
PressurePRESSURE
Using demands, threats, or repeated checking to reach a goal; a more coercive tactic that can have negative effects, so use it with caution.
CoalitionsCOALITIONS
Enlisting the support of others to strengthen influence and reach goals through collective strength; the interests of all parties must be well managed.

Case: A New Manager Leading a Former Boss

CASE ── MANAGING A FORMER BOSS
[ READ FIRST ]
CHEERS 2026
A newly promoted manager leads a key project. The team includes B, a former boss moved from management back to a staff role. B is proud and strict; when the manager tactfully raises a team problem, B gets angry and lectures, and the manager ends up apologizing. The manager holds the formal position; B keeps the experience and prestige.

+ [ 01/ASSESS ]

Assess the problem

  • B's reaction is an emotional issue tied to self-esteem; set aside repairing the relationship for now
  • If the manager wavers, other team members will feel uneasy too
+ [ 02/BOUNDARY ]

Draw boundaries

  • Method 1: Neither cater to B nor speak from a low position; stand firm
  • Method 2: Each person fulfills a role; stress role responsibilities, not rank
  • Method 3: Instruct without hurting B's self-esteem, combining B's experience with the manager's perspective
+ [ 03/CONNECT ]

Find points of connection

  • Situation 1: Start from common ground; first thank B for past guidance and training
  • Situation 2: If B will not cooperate, move from appreciation and respect to consulting and requesting; show some reliance on B
  • Situation 3: If B refuses to listen, introduce changes gradually: try once, then discuss
  • Situation 4: If B insists on the old way, note that greater efficiency means less work; offer support at first
  • Situation 5: If nothing improves, share information with HR or senior management so the manager is not isolated
For discussion: in each approach, whose power is being used, and which tactic?
The approaches follow the paragraph order of the three-page article. The case is set in Japanese firms that move managers back to regular staff roles; in Taiwan, the closer equivalent is a manager brought in from outside, or a young manager leading senior technical staff.

CULTURAL PREFERENCES
[ 2 VIEWS ]

Individualist cultures

INDIVIDUALIST CULTURES

People in individualist countries tend to see power in personalized terms, as a legitimate means of advancing their personal ends. Before using power, understand the target's cultural context.

Collectivist cultures

COLLECTIVIST CULTURES

People in collectivist countries see power in social terms, as a legitimate means of helping others. The same tactic can be seen as more or less legitimate in different cultures.

Applying Power Tactics: Political Skill

POLITICAL SKILL
[ 3 POINTS ]
+ [ 01/DEFINITION ]

Definition

POLITICAL SKILL

Political skill is the ability to influence others in such a way as to enhance one's objectives. Politically skilled people build self-efficacy and networks and achieve higher performance and productivity.

+ [ 02/FEATURE ]

Key feature

UNDETECTED INFLUENCE

Political skill works when others do not notice it. Once it is seen as deliberate manipulation, its effect drops sharply.

+ [ 03/CONTEXT ]

Organizational context

ORGANIZATIONAL CONTEXT

When an organization supports open and fair use of rules and procedures, political skill tends to be negatively related to job performance ratings.

SEXUAL HARASSMENT
[ 2 POINTS ]
+ [ 01/DEFINITION ]

Definition

SEXUAL HARASSMENT

Sexual harassment is any unwanted activity of a sexual nature that affects an individual's employment and creates a hostile work environment. At its core, it is an abuse of unequal power at work.

A manager's basic responsibility is to protect employees from a hostile work environment. Managers may not easily detect who is being harassed, but that does not excuse them from protecting employees and the organization.

Politics: Power in Action

POLITICS: POWER IN ACTION
[ 3 POINTS ]
+ [ 01/DEFINITION ]

Definition

POLITICAL BEHAVIOR

Political behavior consists of activities that are not required as part of an individual's formal role but that influence, or attempt to influence, the distribution of advantages and disadvantages within the organization.

+ [ 02/FEATURES ]

Features

CHARACTERISTICS
  1. Goes beyond the person's job requirements
  2. Arises from an attempt to use one's own power bases
  3. Includes withholding information, forming coalitions, whistleblowing, and spreading rumors
+ [ 03/SKILL ]

Political skill

POLITICAL SKILL

The Reality of Politics

THE REALITY OF POLITICS
[ 3 POINTS ]
+ [ 01/MANAGERS ]

Managers' view

Most managers believe political behavior is an important part of organizational life. As long as it does not directly harm anyone, many managers use some political behavior for ethical reasons or out of necessity. Most also agree that moving up is hard without political skill.

+ [ 02/RESOURCES ]

Resource allocation

The most important factor behind organizational politics is that the facts used to allocate limited resources are open to interpretation. Limited resources and ambiguous standards breed political behavior.

+ [ 03/POLITICKING ]

Self-interest

Organization members use whatever influence they can to interpret the facts in ways that support their own goals and interests. This is called politicking.

Factors Contributing to Political Behavior

CAUSES OF POLITICAL BEHAVIOR
[ 2 SOURCES ]
+ [ 01/INDIVIDUAL ]

Individual factors

INDIVIDUAL FACTORS

High self-monitoring, internal locus of control, high Mach personality, organizational investment, perceived job alternatives, and expectations of success. The higher the sunk costs in the organization and the fewer the outside options, the more likely people are to protect themselves through politics.

+ [ 02/ORGANIZATIONAL ]

Organizational factors

ORGANIZATIONAL FACTORS

Reallocation of resources, promotion opportunities, low trust, role ambiguity, unclear performance evaluation systems, zero-sum reward practices, democratic decision making, high performance pressures, and self-serving senior managers. The less transparent the systems, the greater the payoff from politics.

How Do People Respond to Organizational Politics?

RESPONSES TO ORGANIZATIONAL POLITICS
[ 4 FINDINGS ]
+ [ 01/PERFORMANCE ]

Declining performance

Political manipulation can lower employee performance, perhaps because employees perceive political environments as unfair, which demotivates them and makes them increasingly passive.

+ [ 02/PERCEPTION ]

Understanding

A person's understanding of the hows and whys of organizational politics affects the link between political behavior and performance. People who understand politics are less likely to be hurt by it.

+ [ 03/ETHICAL ]

Ethical leadership

Workplace politics moderates the effects of ethical leadership: in a highly political climate, the positive effects of ethical leadership are diluted.

+ [ 04/DEFENSIVE ]

Defensive behaviors

When employees see politics as a threat, they often respond with defensive behaviors: reactive and protective behaviors to avoid action, blame, or change.

IMPRESSION MANAGEMENT
[ 3 POINTS ]
+ [ 01/DEFINITION ]

Definition

IMPRESSION MANAGEMENT, IM

Impression management (IM) is the process by which individuals attempt to control the impression others form of them. Interviews, presentations, and appraisal meetings are where IM matters most.

+ [ 02/FEATURES ]

Features

CHARACTERISTICS

High self-monitors are more likely to engage in IM. The messages IM conveys may well be true in other circumstances; impression management does not necessarily mean deception.

+ [ 03/RISK ]

RISKS

Misrepresentation carries a high cost. Impression managers must be careful not to be perceived as insincere or manipulative.

Research: Open-Ended Personality Tests Are Harder to Fake

FAKING ON OPEN-ENDED PERSONALITY ASSESSMENTS ── SPEER ET AL.
[ JAP 2026 ]
N=2,039/7 LLMS
Applicant impression management inflates personality scale scores. The same people responded under honest and applicant instructions: scale scores rose by d̄ = .65, but NLP-scored open-ended writing rose only d̄ = .26. AI-written answers usually outscored humans, yet a custom detection model caught them almost perfectly (1% error).
+[ 01/PUZZLE ]

How faking happens in writing

  • Faking on self-report scales is an old selection problem; open-ended tests, where people describe themselves in writing, are thought harder to fake
  • The paper proposes two forms of written IM: elaborate responding and selective disclosure (telling only the good)
  • Among the eight IM techniques, selective disclosure is the written form of self-promotion and enhancement
[ 02/DESIGN ]

Data and methods

  • 2,039 adults were randomly assigned to honest or applicant instructions (applying for a job they really want), then retook the scales honestly 3 to 7 days later
  • Experts rated 466 written responses for elaborate responding and selective disclosure; all responses were NLP-scored
  • Answers generated by seven large language models, and human-AI rewrites, were also compared
[ 03/FINDINGS ]

Key findings

  • Applicant instructions raised elaborate responding (d = .29) and selective disclosure (d = .72); people higher on a trait elaborated and cherry-picked more
  • Faking inflation: scales d̄ = .65, open-ended d̄ = .26; with a scoring model trained on human ratings, it returned to .63
  • Training on mixed honest and applicant responses resisted faking best; AI answers scored high but were detectable
[ 04/IMPLICATIONS ]

Implications for selection and IM

  • IM will not disappear, but item format and scoring can reduce its payoff
  • Faking resistance comes from the design of the scoring model; the item format alone does not provide it. A model rewards whatever it learns
  • AI ghostwriting is a new IM channel; a detection layer is essential for fair selection
The payoff from faking depends on scoring design: NLP-scored open-ended writing cuts it to about 40% of the payoff on scales

Research: Amount of Ingratiation and Third-Party Reactions

INGRATIATION ── AMOUNT, ATTRIBUTION, AND THIRD PARTIES
[ 2021–2025 ]
5 STUDIES
Ingratiation, commonly called "sucking up," is the most common form of impression management. Five studies from the past five years examine how much ingratiation and who is watching. Results agree: supervisor relationship quality first rises, then falls, depending on how the supervisor reads the motive, and supervisors and coworkers judge the same behavior in opposite directions.
+[ 01/THEORY ]

Theory

  • Ingratiation is a form of IM: win liking first, then gain resources and opportunities
  • Recent studies focus on how supervisors interpret motives, based on attribution theory
  • Supervisor side: affective events theory (the supervisor's immediate emotions); coworker side: social comparison (whose standing drops)
[ 02/DESIGN ]

Research design

  • Wang et al. (2025): newcomer-supervisor matched data from three field studies; curvilinear regression tested the rise-then-fall pattern
  • Wu et al. (2025): a scale separating moderate from excessive ingratiation, two experiments, and ten weeks of weekly diaries from supervisors and newcomers
  • The other three: a time-lagged survey plus scenario experiment, a multiwave survey of 64 teams, and 252 supervisor-subordinate dyads
[ 03/FINDINGS ]

Key findings

  • Amount: more ingratiation reads as relationship building; when too obvious, it raises suspicion, so quality rises, then falls
  • Supervisors: excessive ingratiation embarrasses them and they avoid interaction; moderate ingratiation makes them proud and willing to share information. Narcissistic supervisors tolerate more excess
  • Coworkers: feeling their status threatened, they ostracize the ingratiator. Out-ingratiating coworkers improves the supervisor relationship; similar levels keep team relations intact
[ 04/IMPLICATIONS ]

Practical implications

  • Individuals: the problem is ingratiation that is too obvious; frequency matters less
  • Supervisors: rate relationship quality and work output separately in appraisals; narcissistic supervisors tend to relax standards unawares
  • Organizations: the cost falls mainly on coworkers who watch, so system design should account for relative levels within the team
Ingratiation and supervisor relationship quality follow a rise-then-fall pattern, shaped by the supervisor's motive attributions and coworkers' social comparison

Ethics of Political Behavior

THE ETHICS OF BEHAVING POLITICALLY
[ 3 QUESTIONS ]
+ [ 01/UTILITY ]

Utility

What is the utility of engaging in the political behavior? Do the overall benefits outweigh the resources and trust it consumes?

+ [ 02/HARM ]

Harm

How does the utility of the political behavior balance out any harm, or potential harm, it will do to others? If the benefits go to oneself and the harm falls on others, it is ethically indefensible.

+ [ 03/JUSTICE ]

Justice

Does the political activity conform to standards of equity and justice? Political behavior is defensible only if all three questions can be answered with confidence.

Mapping Your Political Career

MAPPING YOUR POLITICAL CAREER
[ 3 STEPS ]
Hard work is only the ticket to entry, since competitors also work hard. Politics is one of the key factors that let hard work pay off. A political map can chart the network of relationships with the key people a career depends on.
+ [ 01/AMBITIONS ]

Clarify ambitions

What are your career aspirations? Who has the power to help you achieve them? Write down the goals and the key people first; only then can the map be drawn.

+ [ 02/RELATIONS ]

Take stock of relationships

What is your current relationship with each key person: close, loose, or none at all? An honest inventory shows which connections to develop.

+ [ 03/INDIRECT ]

Indirect influence

One of the best ways to influence people is indirectly: through other members of the key people's networks, such as former coworkers and friends, to build influence and closer ties.

Implications for Managers

IMPLICATIONS FOR MANAGERS
[ 4 POINTS ]
+ [ 01/MAXIMIZE ]

Maximize your power

Maximize your own power and increase others' dependence on you, for example by developing knowledge or skills your boss needs and being seen as irreplaceable.

+ [ 02/BALANCE ]

Balance of power

Building a power base is not one-way: while you try to minimize your dependence on others and increase theirs on you, employees and colleagues are trying to do the same.

+ [ 03/EMPOWER ]

Avoid disempowering others

Avoid putting others in a position where they feel they have no power.

+ [ 04/FRAME ]

Assess through a political lens

Assessing others in a political framework helps you predict their behavior more accurately and use that information to form political strategies that benefit you and your work unit.
